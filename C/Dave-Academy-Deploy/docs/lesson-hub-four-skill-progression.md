# Lesson Hub Rule — Four-Skill Progression (Permanent)

Permanent curriculum rule for Dave English Academy. Applies to every future
lesson unless Dave explicitly changes it.

## Core principle

The curriculum becomes gradually more advanced. Do not make every lesson use
the same template. Difficulty and lesson structure evolve naturally across
the 100-lesson curriculum. **The curriculum is exactly 100 continuous lessons
- every number 1-100 is a real teaching lesson (including workshop/speaking-
circle lessons); there are no reserved Review or Test lesson numbers and no
gaps.** Reviews, quizzes, and exams are teacher-scheduled events, not
curriculum lessons.

## CEFR targets per band

| Lessons | Band | Target | Focus |
| ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–20 | Foundation | A0 → A1 | Basic vocabulary, greetings, numbers, family, classroom English, simple grammar, confidence speaking |
| 21–40 | Elementary | A1 | Everyday conversations, routines, hobbies, shopping, simple reading and writing |
| 41–60 | Pre-Intermediate | Strong A1 | More vocabulary, past tense, longer dialogues, short reading passages, guided writing |
| 61–80 | Strong A2 | A1+ / Early A2 → A2 | More natural conversations, opinions, problem solving, longer reading, better listening |
| 81–100 | Beginning B1 | A2 → Strong A2/B1 | Richer vocabulary, paragraph writing, longer listening, integrated communication tasks, realistic communication preparing students for the next course |

By the end of Lesson 100, students should realistically reach approximately
**Strong A2 / Beginning B1**, assuming regular attendance and completed
homework. Do not claim 100 lessons alone produce Upper-Intermediate (B2) —
that is not a realistic target.

## Lessons 1–20 (Foundation)

Focus on: Vocabulary, Grammar, Pronunciation, Speaking practice, Simple
exercises, Short writing (words or simple sentences), Homework.

Do not include dedicated Reading or Listening sections.

## Lessons 21–30 (Reading Begins)

Continue everything from Foundation. Add:
- A short Reading passage (~50–100 words)
- 3–5 comprehension questions

Listening is still optional and should generally not be included yet.

## Lessons 31–40 (Listening Begins)

Continue all previous sections. Add:
- A Listening section
- Audio provided through a QR code or external audio link, not embedded in
  the PDF
- Keep listening activities short and beginner-friendly

## Lessons 41–60 (Balanced Four Skills)

Every lesson should normally include: Warm-up, Vocabulary, Grammar, Reading,
Listening, Speaking, Writing, Homework.

## Lessons 61–80 (Intermediate Development)

Increase the complexity of Reading passages, Listening tasks, Speaking
activities, Writing assignments. Students should begin using English more
independently.

## Lessons 81–100 (Confident Communication / Real-World English)

Use authentic, real-life English whenever appropriate. Include: longer
readings, more natural listening tasks, opinion speaking, emails/messages,
longer writing activities, and - toward the end of this band - integrated
real-world tasks: projects, presentations, discussions, multi-skill
activities. Students should demonstrate integrated language skills by
Lesson 100.

## Important rules

- Never suddenly make lessons much harder — increase difficulty gradually.
  Do not design Lesson 60 like Lesson 95; each lesson should feel only a
  small step above the previous one, smooth from Lesson 1 through 100.
- Each lesson should feel like a natural continuation of the previous one.
- Maintain the same high-quality visual design and PDF layout already
  established (themed background/world per lesson, bilingual EN/UZ content)
  — see the Design Lock rule in `CLAUDE.md`. Only educational content
  evolves per this progression; the visual design never does.
- Never redesign earlier lessons to match later ones. Earlier lessons are
  intentionally simpler because they're designed for beginners.

## Quality checklist before marking a lesson complete

- Design matches previous lessons (locked template, unmodified CSS).
- The lesson fits its position in the curriculum (right skills for its band).
- Difficulty is only a small step above the previous lesson.
- The lesson prepares students for the next stage without overwhelming them.
